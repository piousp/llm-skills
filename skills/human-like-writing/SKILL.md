# Human-Like Writing

Goal: text that reads as written by a person, not generated by a model.
The rules apply to every kind of writing: code comments, PR reviews,
summaries, edits, emails, academic prose. Nothing here is domain-specific.

**Working language.** If the working language is Spanish, read `SKILL.es.md`
and follow it instead. That version carries its own banned list and its own
examples.

## Before writing

- [MUST] read `references/banned-list-en.md` and keep it open while drafting.
- [MUST] read `references/voice-en.md` to lock the voice and gather few-shot samples.
- [DO] set the voice and collect 3 to 5 real samples of the author's writing
  before drafting anything longer than one sentence.

## Hard rules

Absolute. Breaking one means a rewrite.

- **[NEVER]** use an em dash to add emphasis or to connect ideas. Use a
  period, a comma, or a semicolon. The em dash is the single strongest
  marker of generated prose.
- **[NEVER]** close with a formula phrase. The last sentence of the text is
  the last concrete point, not a summary of the text. The banned closers
  are listed in `references/banned-list-en.md`.
- **[NEVER]** open a sentence with a filler transition that adds no logic.
  If the transition does no work, cut it or merge the sentence into the
  previous one.
- **[NEVER]** add a template section such as "Challenges and Future
  Directions" or "Key Takeaways" unless the task asks for it by name.
- **[NEVER]** bold headers inside lists, or any systematic formatting. One
  bold word for emphasis at most, and rarely. A plain list already carries
  its own structure.
- **[NEVER]** stack hedges. "It seems this might possibly be somewhat
  risky" reads as evasion. One hedge per claim, or none.
- **[NEVER]** use words from `references/banned-list-en.md`. If a banned
  word is the exact right term, rewrite the sentence around it.

## Soft rules

Habits with judgment, not a checklist.

- **[ALWAYS]** vary sentence length and rhythm. Follow a long sentence with
  a short one. Or 2 short setences, or 2 long ones. Vary the variation.
  Start some sentences with the verb, some with the subject,
  some with a subordinate clause. Generated prose has low burstiness
  (uniform rhythm) and low perplexity (too-predictable word choice);
  detectors combine both signals with vocabulary markers, so
  synonym-swapping does not fool them.
- **[ALWAYS]** take a stance. Say what is wrong, what is good, what should
  change. A neutral summary reads as machine-made. A clear opinion, even a
  mild one, reads as human. "The paper reports a 12% gain, but the control
  group was small" is a stance; "The paper reports a 12% gain" is not.
- **[ALWAYS]** be concrete. Name the file, the line, the number, the date,
  the person. "The merge takes 40 seconds" carries weight; "the merge is
  slow" does not. "The effect held in 14 of 16 trials" beats "the results
  were significant".
- **[DO]** add sensory or lived detail when the genre allows: what the
  build log showed, how the argument sounds read aloud, which test failed
  first. Academic prose takes detail as data. Emails and reviews take it
  as voice.
- **[DO]** cut filler. Every sentence should do work. Read the draft aloud
  and trim what stalls the breath.

## Workflow

Adapt the steps to the size of the task. Never force the full sequence on a
single comment.

**Long or delicate text** (a PR review, a summary, an academic paragraph):

1. **Voice-lock.** Write a one-line voice description, for example "direct,
   specific, mildly opinionated, no hype". Keep it in view.
2. **Outline.** Only if the text will run past a paragraph. Three to five
   bullet points in order. No prose yet.
3. **Draft.** Write freely against the outline, with the banned list and
   the voice description open.
4. **Critique-only.** Read the draft and list what breaks the rules: where
   the rhythm is flat, where the stance hides, which banned word slipped
   in. Diagnose only. Do not rewrite yet.
5. **Rewrite edit-first.** Fix the draft sentence by sentence, starting
   from the critique. Preserve meaning and detail. Never add new arguments
   to pad the text.

**Short text** (a code comment, one reply, a subject line): skip the steps.
Apply the hard and soft rules directly, in one pass.

## Self-check before delivery

A quick mental pass. Answer these questions, fix what fails, then ship.

- Any em dash in the text?
- Any word from the banned list?
- Does it end with a formula closer or a meta-comment?
- Do sentence lengths vary, or does every sentence run the same shape?
- Is the stance visible, or could a neutral reader not tell what the author thinks?
- Is every claim concrete, with a name, a number, or an example behind it?
- Would the author recognize their own voice in this text?
